# Entrepreneurship Craft Guidance

## Source and interpretation

This reference summarizes Shepherd, D. A., and Wiklund, J. (2020), “Simple Rules, Templates, and Heuristics! An Attempt to Deconstruct the Craft of Writing an Entrepreneurship Paper,” *Entrepreneurship Theory and Practice*, 44(3), 371–390, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/1042258719845888.

Label these ideas `[ENT-CRAFT-2020]`. The authors present them as experience-based advice, not journal policy. Apply them conditionally and subordinate them to current target-journal requirements, article type, paradigm, and the evidence.

## Ten conditional heuristics

1. **Establish entrepreneurial relevance**: explain the entrepreneurial phenomenon and its importance beyond a narrow specialist audience.
2. **Clarify the literature architecture**: identify the focal entrepreneurship conversation and the theoretical resources used to advance it. Treat a two-literature structure as optional.
3. **Build rather than caricature**: acknowledge what prior work accomplished before identifying a precise limitation or new opportunity.
4. **Tell an explanatory story**: organize the theory section around the author's mechanism or generative explanation rather than a sequence of article summaries.
5. **Cite strategically but substantively**: include foundational work, current conversation partners, relevant recent developments, appropriate levels of analysis, and competing evidence. Never cite a target journal merely to flatter it.
6. **Theorize context**: decide whether context belongs in the foreground or background by testing its effect on meaning, mechanism, boundary, measurement, time, and relevance.
7. **Use method-appropriate exemplars**: borrow navigation and disclosure patterns, not wording, claims, citations, or intellectual content.
8. **Use figures when they reduce inferential burden**: show a conceptual model, process, nonlinear form, configuration, multilevel structure, data-generation process, or unfamiliar analytic result only when the figure adds information.
9. **Show how understanding changes**: make the discussion explain new insight rather than repeat results or introduce unrelated theories.
10. **Rewrite for readers**: improve clarity, coherence, terminology, transitions, and evidence signaling through iterative revision.

## Section architecture for deductive empirical papers

Use this as a diagnostic scaffold, not a mandatory outline.

### Introduction

1. Establish the consequential entrepreneurial phenomenon.
2. Define the live scholarly conversation and what is known.
3. Surface a non-obvious puzzle, conflict, inadequacy, or boundary.
4. Explain why resolving it matters.
5. State the research question and theoretical approach.
6. Preview the design only to the extent needed to establish leverage.
7. State a small set of bounded contributions as changes in understanding, not a list of added variables.

### Theory and hypotheses

1. Define constructs and levels consistently.
2. Present the overarching mechanism or model before detailed links when appropriate.
3. Develop each prediction from premises, mechanism, boundary, and plausible rival.
4. Avoid a separate theory for every arrow and avoid citation chains without explanation.
5. Connect theoretical constructs to the empirical indicators without substituting measures for constructs.

### Methods

1. Explain why the setting, sample, timing, and design can answer the question.
2. Disclose sampling, exclusions, missingness, attrition, procedures, ethics, and data provenance.
3. Justify construct operationalization, adaptations, translations, and validation.
4. Explain model choices, controls, uncertainty, robustness logic, and exploratory departures.

### Results

1. Report descriptive evidence and the analytic sample clearly.
2. Link each test to the stated claim and report supported, null, and contrary results.
3. Report uncertainty and magnitude, not threshold significance alone.
4. Interpret interactions, nonlinearities, configurations, mediation, and process claims with appropriate quantities and figures.
5. Distinguish planned tests, robustness checks, and post hoc exploration.

### Discussion

1. Return to the motivating phenomenon and question.
2. Synthesize findings rather than recite hypothesis outcomes.
3. Develop the few deepest changes to the focal entrepreneurship conversation.
4. Explain what the entrepreneurial setting changes or exports to broader theory.
5. Bound claims honestly and treat unexpected findings as post hoc unless preregistered or theorized in advance.
6. Derive practical implications from the actual evidence.
7. Use limitations to define boundaries and productive next questions, not as ritual disclaimers.

### Conclusion

Use a short conclusion only when it adds a memorable bounded insight. Do not repair an underdeveloped discussion with a rhetorical ending.

## Writing heuristics

- Rewrite for argument clarity, not surface elegance alone.
- Prefer active voice when it clarifies who theorizes, acts, observes, or decides; retain passive voice when the actor is genuinely irrelevant.
- Connect sentences and paragraphs with substantive transitions that state the logical relation.
- Use concrete or hypothetical examples only when they clarify a construct or mechanism without creating a competing interpretation.
- Keep list elements grammatically and conceptually parallel.

## Anti-formula warnings

- Do not require exactly two literatures, three contributions, one theory, or a conceptual figure.
- Do not treat “recent citations from the target journal” as an acceptance tactic.
- Do not force hypotheses into inductive, abductive, conceptual, or review papers.
- Do not assume context matters only when it reverses an effect.
- Do not equate a smooth story with a credible explanation.
