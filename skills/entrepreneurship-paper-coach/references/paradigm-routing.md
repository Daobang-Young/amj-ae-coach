# Paradigm and Article-Type Routing

Select only the branch that matches the study's purpose and evidence. If the manuscript combines branches, state which inference belongs to each.

## Deductive quantitative empirical work

Audit:

- phenomenon-question-theory alignment;
- construct definitions before measures;
- units, levels, timing, and functional form;
- temporal order and causal language;
- sample and setting fit;
- measurement validity and invariance where relevant;
- theoretically justified controls and avoidance of post-treatment controls;
- estimator fit, uncertainty, clustering, missingness, selection, and attrition;
- measured mechanisms versus mechanism-consistent patterns;
- rival explanations, sensitivity, falsification, and practical magnitude.

Treat cross-sectional mediation, change, learning, emergence, and process claims as major mismatches unless the claim is narrowed and limitations are explicit.

## Inductive qualitative work

Audit:

- why the phenomenon requires discovery rather than prior hypothesis testing;
- case/site selection and theoretical sampling logic;
- access, researcher role, reflexivity, ethics, and the temporal research journey;
- evidence provenance, coding evolution, negative cases, and alternative interpretations;
- data displays that connect raw evidence, categories, process, and emergent theory;
- whether the back end interweaves evidence and theory rather than applying an imported template after the fact;
- transferability and theoretical boundaries rather than statistical generalization.

Do not demand a deductive two-literature structure or front-loaded hypotheses.

## Abductive and process work

Audit:

- the anomaly or surprise that initiated iteration;
- temporal bracketing, event sequence, turning points, and path dependence;
- cycles between data and theory and a transparent account of major shifts;
- rival process explanations and evidence for sequencing or generative mechanisms;
- whether the final model preserves temporal complexity without becoming an unfalsifiable story.

## Mixed-method and multiple-study work

Audit:

- the unique inferential job of each study or method;
- ordering and integration logic;
- whether weaknesses are genuinely offset rather than repeated;
- construct and sample comparability;
- convergence, complementarity, or principled divergence;
- whether the combined claim exceeds what any component can support.

Do not require multiple studies mechanically. Require them only when they materially strengthen the central inference.

## Conceptual entrepreneurship papers

Audit:

- whether the target journal accepts conceptual work;
- the focal entrepreneurial phenomenon or theoretical problem;
- construct clarity and logical consistency;
- assumptions, levels, boundary conditions, and explanatory mechanism;
- genuine theory change rather than relabeling, typology inflation, or variable addition;
- propositions only when they sharpen the argument;
- generativity: new explanations, questions, or research programs that follow.

For AMJ, route purely conceptual work away from AMJ under current mission rules. Consider journal-specific alternatives only after checking their current scope.

## Reviews and research agendas

Audit:

- review type and fit to the question;
- transparent search, screening, coding, and synthesis boundaries;
- coverage of adjacent and contradictory evidence;
- synthesis that produces an explanatory framework or changed understanding rather than a catalog;
- research questions derived from theoretical tensions and evidence limits, not from empty cells alone.

## Methods and design-science work

Audit:

- whether the method or artifact addresses a consequential entrepreneurship problem;
- the design principles, assumptions, evaluation criteria, and iteration history;
- empirical demonstration of what becomes inferentially or practically possible;
- theory implications beyond technical novelty;
- target-journal acceptance of the article type.

## Claim-language guardrail

Use the design to cap language:

| Evidence supports | Prefer | Avoid without stronger leverage |
|---|---|---|
| Description | “documents,” “characterizes,” “is observed” | “explains,” “drives” |
| Association | “is associated with,” “covaries with” | “causes,” “leads to” |
| Prediction | “predicts out of sample” | causal mechanism claims |
| Mechanism-consistent evidence | “is consistent with” | “demonstrates the mechanism” |
| Identified causal effect | bounded causal wording tied to assumptions | universal or mechanism claims not tested |
| Qualitative process evidence | “develops a process explanation,” with boundaries | statistical prevalence or population effect claims |
