# Current AMJ Policy and AI Responsibilities

Verified: **2026-07-03 (Asia/Shanghai)**. Recheck live official pages before any pre-submission verdict because requirements can change.

## Current AMJ mission and submission screen `[AMJ-CURRENT]`

Official page: https://www.aom.org/publications/journals/publishing-with-aom/author-and-reviewer-resources/author-resources/submitting-to-journal/

- AMJ publishes empirical research that tests, extends, or builds management theory and informs practice.
- It welcomes qualitative, quantitative, field, laboratory, meta-analytic, mixed, and other empirical methods.
- A submission needs strong empirical and theoretical contributions and should make practical relevance clear to a broad management readership.
- Purely conceptual manuscripts belong elsewhere in the AOM portfolio.
- The desk screen considers mission fit and whether the paper has at least a minimal likelihood of favorable reviewer evaluation.
- The current page says manuscripts are evaluated for contribution-to-length and typically should not exceed 40 double-spaced pages, inclusive of all content; longer papers may be considered when warranted.
- Blind review must not be compromised. Treat all author-identifying material and acknowledgments accordingly.

Do not turn “typically 40 pages” into an automatic rejection rule. Recheck the live page and distinguish a formatting risk from a scholarly weakness.

## Current AOM style `[AMJ-CURRENT]`

Official page: https://www.aom.org/publications/journals/publishing-with-aom/author-and-reviewer-resources/author-resources/author-resources-editorial-style-guides/

At the verification date, the web guide specifies, among other items:

- Word document; US Letter; Times New Roman 12-point; double-spaced body; one-inch margins.
- Abstract no longer than 200 words.
- Three unnumbered heading levels with prescribed styling.
- Define technical terms; avoid concept abbreviations; use one variable name consistently across text, tables, figures, and appendices.
- State each tested hypothesis separately, in present tense, with a distinct label and prescribed block styling.

Use the live guide, not this summary, for final formatting.

## Selected later AMJ editorials

- `[AMJ-FTE-2021]` Tihanyi & DeCelles, “Publishing Original Research in AMJ,” DOI: https://doi.org/10.5465/amj.2021.4003. Check originality of data, ideas, and text; disclose related papers/data overlap so editors can evaluate it.
- `[AMJ-FTE-2021]` DeCelles, Howard-Grenville, & Tihanyi, “Improving the Transparency of Empirical Research Published in AMJ,” DOI: https://doi.org/10.5465/amj.2021.4004. Contribution depends on trustworthy evidence; describe how research and analyses evolved, show inconvenient details, and use supplements judiciously. Transparency does not substitute for originality.
- `[AMJ-FTE-2024]` Bliese et al., “Strengthening Theory–Methods–Data Links,” DOI: https://doi.org/10.5465/amj.2024.4004. Treat theory, method, and data as an interdependent chain; specify assumptions, boundary conditions, time, measurement, and the inferential role of the design.
- `[AMJ-FTE-2025]` Grégoire et al., “Compelling Empirics in Quantitative Studies,” DOI: https://doi.org/10.5465/amj.2025.4004. Make empirical choices serve the theory; justify context/data/variables/analysis, respect temporal order, measure mechanisms carefully, address plausible confounds, and communicate effect meaning.
- `[AMJ-FTE-2026]` Roberson, “Artificial Intelligence and Responsible Research at AMJ,” DOI: https://doi.org/10.5465/amj.2026.4002. AI is assistance, not authorship; responsibility, privacy, transparency, and human scholarly judgment remain with authors.

These editorials guide diagnosis but are not a hidden scoring model.

## AI policy `[AMJ-CURRENT]`

Official page: https://www.aom.org/publications/journals/publishing-with-aom/aom-artificial-intelligence-policy/

At the verification date, authors must:

1. retain responsibility for manuscript accuracy and research integrity;
2. disclose AI use at each research stage and confirm careful review, verification, and acceptance of AI-involved output;
3. identify model and purpose in the cover letter and acknowledgments;
4. verify AI-generated content and citations, provide the underlying sources, check plagiarism risk, and acknowledge relevant limitations.

The five listed disclosure stages are:

1. Conceptualization
2. Research Design
3. Data Preparation and Analysis
4. Presentation of Results
5. Writing and Editing

Maintain this author-side log:

| Stage | AI used? | Model/version | Date | Exact purpose | Inputs/data sensitivity | Output retained | Human checks performed | Author accepted? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

For this skill, at minimum log `Conceptualization` when it shapes framing/theory and `Writing and Editing` when candidate prose is produced. Add other stages only when actually used.
